What to do

Turn a bag that holds dice into a bag that has rules about dice, then do the same thing to a deck of your own cards.

  • Make DiceBag's list private and hand out IReadOnlyList<Dice>, then delete the two scenes that stop compiling.
  • Make Draw remove what it draws, and be honest when the bag is empty.
  • Fix Shuffle so the bag itself changes, and make DrawnCount { get; private set; }.
  • Rebuild Standard() out of Add() calls, so the factory comes through the same door as everybody else.
  • Build a Deck of your own cards that shuffles, deals, and runs out honestly -- and a Hand that refuses a sixth card.
  • Score one five-card hand with both Lesson 3 rule sets, and show the two answers side by side.

What to turn in

  1. Your repository URL, with Lesson04_Collections committed and pushed.
  2. Your Deck.cs and Hand.cs.
  3. The output of one five-card hand scored two ways.
  4. Your two-column list from Step 1 -- every rule the starter tried to enforce, and how a caller got around it.
  5. Your answer to Show your thinking below.

Getting your URL from GitHub Desktop: type a short summary, click Commit to main, then click Push origin at the top.  Both steps matter -- a commit that has yet to be pushed stays on your laptop, and the page I open would show your work from last week.  Then Repository > View on GitHub and copy the address out of your browser.  The bin/ and obj/ folders stay out of the repository on their own, so leave them alone.  If pushing refuses to work, zip the project folder instead and tell me what it said.
